# 10-User Paid Alpha Plan

This plan is designed to answer one question quickly:

**Will serious creators pay for Album Conceptualizer as the workflow that helps them finish coherent album projects?**

## Goal

Acquire `10` paying alpha users who actively use the product on real album projects and give structured feedback.

Success is not just signups. Success is:

- `10` paying users
- at least `6` who create a real album project
- at least `4` who return weekly for two or more weeks
- at least `3` who export or publish a project

## Who To Recruit

Prioritize creators whose problem is coherence, not instant audio generation.

### Best-fit profiles

- Indie artists planning a concept EP or album
- Producer-songwriters building narrative or thematic releases
- Bands with one clear creative lead
- Soundtrack or theater-adjacent creators working across multiple pieces

### Avoid for the first 10

- users who only want one-click finished songs
- broad "AI music curious" traffic with no album project
- large teams expecting mature admin or enterprise controls
- users who want polished real-time collaboration on day one

## Offer

Use a simple founder offer:

- `Pro` at `$12/mo`
- founder pricing protected for `6-12` months
- direct onboarding help
- direct feedback channel with the team

Do not sell a public Team plan in this phase. If a small band or duo wants it, onboard them manually.

## Core Message

Use this framing consistently:

> Album Conceptualizer helps you turn one idea into a coherent album blueprint with narrative, lyrics, harmony, versions, collaboration, and export-ready handoff.

Do not pitch it as an audio generator.

## Acquisition Channels

Start with channels where intent and taste are already strong.

### Highest-probability channels

- personal network of musicians, producers, and writers
- niche Discord servers for indie artists, production, composition, and songwriting
- Reddit communities where album craft and process matter more than pure AI hype
- direct outreach to artists already posting about concept projects or unfinished albums
- small creator circles, songwriting groups, and local music communities

### Lower-priority channels

- broad paid ads
- generic AI-tool directories
- mass cold outreach with weak personalization

Those channels will produce noise before they produce signal.

## Recruiting Script

Use concise outreach. Example:

> I’m working on a tool for artists and producer-songwriters who want to build concept albums that actually hold together. It helps with album bibles, track planning, lyrics/chords structure, versioning, and DAW handoff. If you’re actively building a multi-song project, I’d like to onboard 10 paid alpha users and work closely with them.

## Onboarding Flow

Each user should get a lightweight, high-touch onboarding flow.

### Step 1: Qualification

Confirm:

- they are actively working on a multi-song project
- they care about coherence across tracks
- they are willing to pay, even at alpha stage
- they will provide feedback

### Step 2: Setup

Get them to:

- sign in
- create their first album
- write a concept summary
- define a tracklist
- fill in at least one album bible section
- edit at least one song in studio

### Step 3: First success milestone

Within the first session, get them to one of:

- a usable album scaffold
- a refined album bible
- a real export pack
- a published/remixed concept

If they do not reach visible momentum quickly, they will churn before the second session.

## Weekly Operating Cadence

### Week 1

- recruit first `5` users
- onboard each one manually
- observe where they stall
- fix obvious onboarding and messaging friction immediately

### Week 2

- recruit the next `5`
- start collecting repeated objections and repeated wins
- tighten upgrade prompts and onboarding copy

### Week 3

- review usage patterns
- identify the 2-3 moments that correlate with retention
- improve those moments directly in product and messaging

### Week 4

- decide whether the product is ready for a broader beta
- keep only the channels and messages that brought the best-fit users
- document testimonials, before/after examples, and strongest proof points

## Feedback Loop

Collect the same data from every alpha user.

### Ask after first session

- What were you trying to make?
- Where did the product feel immediately helpful?
- Where did it feel confusing or thin?
- What almost made you stop?
- What would make this worth paying for long term?

### Ask after one week

- Did you come back on your own?
- Did it improve the project in a way your DAW/notes workflow did not?
- Did you export, publish, or share anything?
- Would you be disappointed if the product disappeared tomorrow?

## Metrics To Track

Track these from day one:

- landing -> signup conversion
- signup -> first album created
- first album -> first studio edit
- first album -> first export
- first album -> publish/remix
- free -> paid conversion
- week-1 retention

If you can only instrument a few events first, prioritize:

- signup
- album created
- first export
- publish
- upgrade

## What To Learn From The First 10

You are trying to validate:

- whether the wedge is strong enough
- whether people care about coherence enough to pay
- whether onboarding gets them to value fast enough
- whether export and publish/remix meaningfully increase retention

You are **not** trying to optimize at scale yet.

## Ship List Before Recruiting Hard

Before pushing hard on acquisition, make sure these are solid:

- homepage messaging clearly explains the wedge
- first-project flow creates momentum in under 5 minutes
- basic funnel analytics are live
- auth, billing, webhook, and export are validated in staging
- there are at least `3-5` strong example albums users can learn from

## Definition of Success

At the end of this alpha, you want to be able to say:

> We found a clear group of creators who will pay for coherence, not just audio generation, and we know which product moments create conviction.

If you cannot say that, do not broaden the launch yet.
